# Group Owner Guide

A Group is your reading community's home in Northbound. It brings together
Members, shared announcements and the Challenges your community runs. As Group
Owner, you manage membership, choose who helps you, and oversee Challenge setup
and results.

Start by opening your Group. **Group Settings**, **Access Code**, **Members** and
**Challenges** are your main destinations. Open an individual Challenge for its
**Challenge Settings**, participants and reporting.

## Choose who helps you

- **Moderators** are permanent Group members with an ongoing support role. Their
  default extra authority is managing Group announcements; give them additional
  Group permissions deliberately if they need them.
- **Hosts** run a particular Challenge. You assign them for that Challenge, without
  changing their permanent Group role.
- **Readers** take part in Challenges and submit their completed books. Joining
  the Group is separate from registering for a Challenge.

**Being Group Owner does not automatically make you a Host.** You can oversee
settings, staffing, planning and scores, but operations such as managing teams,
Themes, BOTM and Games require a Host assignment. Reviewing books also requires
a Challenge review role. Assign yourself as Host if you will do that work.

## Review your Group's details

1. Open **Group Settings**.
2. Review the name and timezone. The timezone matters for Challenge schedules and
   the dates used for Games rewards.
3. To display a shared message, select **Enable Group Announcement** and enter a
   **Group Announcement**. An enabled announcement needs text.
4. Choose **Save**.

You can also use **Edit Group Announcement** on the Group page. A Challenge's own
announcement is managed separately in **Challenge Settings → Challenge Announcement**.

### Invite people with the access code

Open **Access Code** to see **Current Code**. Share it privately with people you
want to join your Group using their Northbound accounts.

Use **Access Code Visibility** to decide who in the Group may see the code, then
**Save Settings**. **Regenerate Code** replaces the old invitation code immediately;
people using the old code can no longer join with it. Existing Members remain.

### Add a new Member yourself

For someone who does not already have an account:

1. Open **Members → Add Member**.
2. Enter a unique **Username**, **Display Name**, optional email and **Role**.
3. Set a **Temporary Password** of at least 12 characters.
4. Choose **Save**, then share the sign-in details privately.

For someone with an existing account, use the Group access code instead of
creating a second account.

## Manage Members and responsibilities

**Members** shows each Member's role, status and summary reading figures. Select
a name to open their Member Profile, including **Verified Pages**, **Approved
Books** and **Months Participated**. Owning the Group does not expose another
Member's full **Monthly History** there.

### Change a role or delegate a specific task

1. Find the Member in **Members** and choose **Permissions**.
2. In **Persistent Group Role**, choose the appropriate role.
3. Use **Delegated Group Permissions** for specific responsibilities. Each setting
   can **Use Role Default**, **Allow** or **Deny**.
4. Choose **Save Group Permissions**.

Delegate only what the person needs: Group settings and integrations, membership,
Challenge creation and Host assignment, announcements, or Group permissions.
A Moderator title alone does not grant all those powers. An explicit permission
override can also change what an Owner is allowed to do.

To share or hand over ownership, give another Member the Group owner role here.
You cannot change your own role or permissions on this screen. Arrange the handoff
first; the other Owner can then change your role. There is no separate ownership
transfer wizard. Granting ownership gives substantial control over the Group.

### Remove a Member carefully

Use **Remove** on the Member's row and read the confirmation before proceeding.
This makes their Group membership inactive; it does not delete their account or
erase recorded books, statistics and team history. You cannot remove yourself.
Someone with a valid access code can rejoin, so replace a widely shared code if
you also need to stop its further use.

Group membership and a Challenge's participant roster are different. Coordinate
any Challenge staffing or roster changes with its Host rather than treating
Group removal as a way to erase participation history.

## Create a Challenge and agree its schedule

1. From the Group, choose **Create Challenge**.
2. Enter a **Challenge Title**.
3. Select a Group member under **Host**, choose **Add Host**, and check
   **Selected Hosts**. Select at least one; include yourself if you will operate it.
4. Choose **Save**. The new Challenge starts in **Draft**, with registration closed
   and dates still to configure.
5. Open the **General** card in **Challenge Settings** to edit the title and
   description; use **Save General Settings**.
6. Open **Challenge Schedule**, set the dates and automation choices, and choose
   **Save Schedule**.

Drafts are preparation spaces, hidden from ordinary Members. Assigned Hosts and
people with Challenge-management authority can work on them before publication.

### Registration and lifecycle are separate

The schedule has **Registration Opens**, **Registration Closes**, **Challenge
Starts**, **Challenge Ends** and **Final Announcement**. Times use the Group's
timezone. Each has its own automatic-action checkbox: entering a time alone is
not enough if its checkbox is off.

With **Open registration automatically** enabled, the opening time opens
registration and moves a Draft to **Upcoming**. **Close registration automatically**
closes it at the configured closing time. Check the Challenge page's displayed
registration status before inviting Readers to register.

You can also publish a Draft early with **Move Forward to Upcoming** under
**Challenge Lifecycle**. That stage change alone does not open registration.
Readers can register only while registration is open and the Challenge is
Upcoming or Active.

The start/end automation moves Upcoming to Active and Active to Finalizing.
**Mark Challenge Completed at this time** uses the Final Announcement time to
complete a Finalizing Challenge; leave it off if you want to finish review before
manually marking the Challenge Completed.

## Staff the Challenge without changing Group roles

Open **Challenge Settings → Hosts**. Use **Assign Host** to add a Member. Review
**Current Hosts**, and use **Remove Host** when their assignment ends. Previous
assignments remain in **Host History**. Assigning a Host does not register that
person as a participant or put them on a team.

Agree with your Hosts how they will staff the event:

- **Hosts** manage Challenge operations and can review throughout the Challenge.
  They can configure the Challenge, but being Host alone does not let them appoint
  other Hosts or manage permanent Group membership.
- **Team Leaders** are competing Readers on their own team, with review authority
  limited to that team. Hosts manage their team assignments and leadership.
- **Floaters** provide Challenge-wide review support as a separate staffing role.
  They are not competing Readers or team members in that Challenge.
- **Readers** register and are assigned to teams separately from staffing.

Ask Hosts to manage **Teams** and **Floaters**. A Group role does not substitute
for any of these Challenge assignments.

## Prepare registration and oversee participants

Use **Challenge Settings → Signup Questions** before collecting registrations.
You and the Hosts can configure up to ten custom questions, choose their types,
mark required answers and provide choices where appropriate. Use **Add Question**,
**Move Up**, **Move Down** and **Remove Question**, then **Save Registration Settings**.
Readers still confirm registration when there are no custom questions.

Set **Reader Answer Editing** to no editing, a set period, or until registration
closes. For a set period, **Editing Duration (Hours)** runs from the Reader's
registration. Once registration responses have been collected, the question
structure and answer-editing policy are locked. Set expectations beforehand.

Use **Challenge Participants** for planning: it shows registration state, team,
**Avg Pages**, **Last Challenge**, **Completed**, and available Discord usernames.
Historical planning figures use approved base pages from eligible Completed
Challenges in this Group, not the current Challenge's bonuses. A missing history
figure is not the same as zero reading.

Hosts handle **Add Participant**, **Edit Team**, participant **Remove** and
**Reactivate**. Removal ends active participation rather than deleting its history;
reactivation brings that participation back. Agree team balancing and participant
changes with them instead of changing permanent Group roles.

Treat registration answers and planning information as private community-management
information. Your operational access to a Discord username does not make it public.
The Reader controls **Make Discord username public**; you do not change that
preference for them.

## Agree scoring and review expectations

Readers submit completed books; authorized reviewers approve the base pages and
applicable claims. Hosts and Floaters review across the Challenge; Team Leaders
review within their team. Ownership or Moderator status alone does not grant
book-review authority. Review takes place in **Active** and **Finalizing**.

Reports keep three ideas separate:

- **Base**: approved reading pages, not simply everything submitted.
- **Modifier**: scoring adjustments, including eligible feature bonuses and Games rewards.
- **Total**: Base plus Modifier—the final score shown when bonuses are included.

Ask Hosts to explain the configured **Themes**, BOTM, Personal TBR and Games before
registration. A pending submission or unapproved claim is not an earned bonus.
Hosts manage Themes and their rewards; Owners oversee the agreed rules rather
than acquiring review rights just by viewing a score.

### Book of the Month

BOTM is an optional Challenge reading list. Hosts enable it through **Challenge
Settings → BOTM Settings**, using **Enable Book of the Month**, and manage the
list through **Books**. Agree the per-book bonuses and **Full BOTM Completion
Bonus Pages** with them; zero can be used for no full-completion bonus.

Hosts can look up titles through the Group's Hardcover catalog connection or
enter them manually. Approved qualifying completions contribute to BOTM progress
and its configured bonuses. Merely putting a title on the list does not award
pages. Disabling BOTM preserves configured books; it is not a way to delete the list.

### Personal TBR

Under **Signup Questions → Personal TBR**, you or a Host can select **Enable
Personal TBR**, configure **Per-book TBR Bonus Pages** and **Full 9-Book TBR
Completion Bonus Pages**, then **Save Personal TBR Settings**.

Readers may prepare up to nine planned books as part of registration. Their TBR
locks when they confirm registration, even if registration answers remain editable.
The full-list bonus requires a nine-book list and qualifying completion of all
nine books. Ask Hosts to oversee completion and review; do not promise Readers
that reopening an answer-editing window will unlock their confirmed list.

### Games and manual rewards

**Games** are Challenge-scoped activities with manual bonus-page rewards. Hosts
enable them in Challenge Settings and use **Apply Reward** for **Individual**,
**Team** or **Challenge-wide** targets. The specified amount applies to each
recipient, not as a pool divided between them.

Hosts supply a **Reason / Note** and confirm the reward. The effective date is
the Group-local date when it is applied, not an Owner-selected backdate. Recipients
are fixed at that time; later team changes do not move the award to new recipients.

Hosts can inspect **Reward History**, including who applied a reward, its reason
and recipients. **Void Reward** removes that reward's scoring effect while retaining
its visible history and void reason. It is not an invisible deletion. As Owner,
agree the reward policy and ask Hosts to resolve mistakes; detailed reward operation
requires Host authority. Disabling Games preserves existing rewards and scores.

## Monitor totals, visibility and progress

Open **Teams**, then a team for its Team Detail report. Owner oversight includes
Reader scores and team standings even when competition visibility is restricted.
Use **Competition Visibility** in Challenge Settings to control **Reader Scores**
and **Team Standings** separately for other viewers. Choices range from **Hosts
only** to **Everybody**; Everybody means Group members, not the public internet.
Team-specific choices limit access to the relevant team.

In Team Detail, **From** and **To** filter reported dates. **Show Bonuses** controls
whether modifiers are included. **Show Readers** can filter the displayed roster
by feature participation; planning-column options expose the available Discord
and reading-history figures. Choose **Apply Filters**, or **Reset Filters** before
comparing a total with another screen.

**Displayed Team Total** reflects the report's filters; **Current Readers** is a
roster count, not a count of only those displayed by a filter. Check Base, Modifier
and Total separately when a score seems unexpected.

**Detailed Reader access is narrower than score visibility.** Selecting a Reader
from a Challenge report opens Participant Detail only for that Reader themselves
or staff with review authority over them. Owner status alone is not sufficient.
If you are also an assigned Host, that page brings together **Score**,
**Planning / History**, **Registration**, Personal TBR, BOTM, Themes,
**Submissions** and **Modifiers / Rewards**. Use that role when you need detailed
review oversight; otherwise coordinate with the Host.

### Progress checkpoints

You or a Host can configure up to five **Progress Checkpoints** in Challenge
Settings. Set **Checkpoint Date and Time**, **Threshold Percentage**, **Progress
Basis** and **Target Basis**, then **Save Checkpoints**. The basis can use base or
scored pages, with a previous-average or fixed page target.

Checkpoints compare active Readers' progress with the chosen target. Below-target
warnings appear in participant planning; missing previous history can mean a Reader
is not evaluated. These checks do not automatically remove Readers or change scores.
Configuration locks once evaluation begins, preserving the results. Checkpoints
missed until after the active competition are skipped rather than inventing a
historical progress result.

## Finish the Challenge deliberately

Use **Challenge Settings → Challenge Lifecycle** for the available adjacent stage
changes. The sequence is **Draft → Upcoming → Active → Finalizing → Completed →
Archived**. Hosts can also move the lifecycle, so agree who makes final decisions.

- In **Finalizing**, finish outstanding review. Many setup controls are no longer
  available; this is not another registration period.
- Move to **Completed** when review and results are ready. Normal review is no
  longer available there.
- Move to **Archived** when you want to put the Challenge away. History is retained,
  but there is no normal lifecycle action to bring an Archived Challenge back.

**Move Back to** actions require confirmation. Returning Completed to Finalizing
uses **Confirm Recovery to Finalizing** and can change results visibility; it
preserves existing records. Read the confirmation rather than using stage changes
as a way to clear results. Also review scheduled automation so it does not move
the Challenge forward again unintentionally.

## Connect the Group to Hardcover, optionally

The Group connection is a **Group-owned scoped API key** for shared catalog,
search and metadata work, including submission and BOTM lookup. It is not a
Reader's personal library connection. Manual book entry remains available without it.

1. Open **Group Settings** and its Hardcover connection section.
2. Use **Connect Hardcover**, or **Replace Hardcover Token** for an existing connection.
3. Enter the Group's scoped **API Token**. Its catalog scopes are
   `read:catalog:data` and `read:catalog:search`.
4. Use **Test Token** to check it or **Save and Test** to save and check it.
5. Review **Working** or **Needs Attention** and **Last Tested**. Use **Test
   Connection** later if catalog lookup stops working.

Keep the key private. Managing it requires Group settings/integration authority;
being a Host or Moderator alone is not enough. **Disconnect**, followed by
**Confirm Disconnect**, stops the Group connection without deleting existing
submissions or cached book information. See the
[Hardcover connection guide](HARDCOVER.md#two-connections-two-owners) for integration details.

### A Reader's Hardcover account remains theirs

You cannot enable another Reader's personal sync, see their token, impersonate
their Hardcover account, or use the Group key as a fallback for their sync.
Readers manage their own connection and consent from **My Account**.

Connecting is not consenting. Synchronization is strictly forward-looking:
turning it on does not queue previously approved Northbound history. Only new
eligible approvals after the Reader enables consent can enter normal sync.
There is no historical-backfill feature, and Hardcover availability does not
determine Northbound approval or scoring.

## A practical first-Challenge checklist

1. Review Group details, timezone, announcement and access-code sharing.
2. Add Members and delegate only the ongoing Group tasks your Moderators need.
3. Create a Draft Challenge with at least one Host.
4. Agree the schedule, registration questions, answer-editing window and visibility.
5. Configure Personal TBR if wanted; ask Hosts to prepare teams, Themes, BOTM and Games.
6. Confirm Hosts, Team Leaders and Floaters understand their separate responsibilities.
7. Let the configured schedule publish/open registration, or publish Upcoming early;
   verify registration actually shows Open before asking Readers to register.
8. Review participant planning with Hosts and let them manage team assignments.
9. During Active, monitor filtered totals, review progress and any checkpoints.
10. Finish review in Finalizing, then complete and eventually archive deliberately.

## Common questions

**A Member cannot see the Challenge.** Confirm they have active Group membership
and that the Challenge is not still Draft. Restricted scores are a separate issue:
check Competition Visibility rather than changing the Member's permanent role.

**A Host cannot see a Group or staffing setting.** A Host assignment is only for
that Challenge. Group membership, integration management and appointing Hosts
need the corresponding Group permission. Check that their Host assignment is
current and for the right Challenge before changing anything else.

**Registration is unavailable.** Check the displayed registration status,
opening/closing times, automatic-action checkboxes and Challenge stage. Upcoming
alone does not mean Open. Answer-editing windows are separate, and they do not
unlock a confirmed Personal TBR.

**I can see a score but cannot open the Reader's details.** This is the separate
review-scope restriction. Work with the Host, or assign yourself Host if that is
part of your agreed responsibility.

**Hardcover search is unavailable.** Check the Group connection's status and use
Test Connection; replace a rejected key through Group Settings. A Reader's Personal
TBR lookup uses their own connection, so replacing the Group key does not fix that.

**Totals differ between screens.** Reset date and Reader filters, check Show
Bonuses, and compare Base with Base or Total with Total. Historical planning
averages are not current Challenge scores.

**A setting has become read-only.** Check the lifecycle and the page's lock message.
Registration responses lock question configuration; checkpoint evaluation locks
checkpoints; later stages restrict normal operations. Do not promise a stage
change will remove every lock.
